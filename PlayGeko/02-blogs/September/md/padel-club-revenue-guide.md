## Introduction

Court bookings are your foundation. But they shouldn't be your only revenue stream.

Most padel clubs generate 80-90% of their revenue from court bookings. That's a fragile business model. When courts are full, you're profitable. When they're not, you're struggling.

The most successful padel clubs diversify their revenue across 7 distinct streams. If you're only relying on one or two, you're leaving significant money on the table.

This guide breaks down each revenue stream, explains how to implement it, and shows the financial impact.

---

## Revenue Stream 1: Coaching Packages

Coaching is the highest-margin revenue stream for most padel clubs. Yet many clubs underinvest in it.

### Why Coaching Matters

A casual player books a court for $25. A player who buys a 10-pack coaching bundle spends $200+ over several weeks. That's 8x the revenue from one player relationship.

Coaching also increases player retention. Players who take lessons stay longer, book more frequently, and spend more overall.

### How to Implement It

**Set up coach profiles.** Each coach sets availability, pricing, and session types.

**Create packages.** Offer 5-pack, 10-pack, and unlimited bundles at different price points.

**Sell online.** Players purchase packages through the app or website.

**Track usage.** Coaches log sessions. Players see their balance. Automated reminders before expiry.

### Revenue Potential

A club with 200 active members and a 15% coaching adoption rate generates $6,000-10,000/month in coaching revenue alone. That's on top of court bookings.

## Revenue Stream 2: Memberships

Memberships create predictable, recurring revenue. They also increase player loyalty and lifetime value.

### Membership Models

**Basic membership.** Access to booking platform, marketplace listing, and member-only events.

**Premium membership.** Discounted court rates, priority booking, free coaching assessment, and pro shop discounts.

**All-access membership.** Unlimited booking (within fair-use limits), coaching discounts, tournament entry, and exclusive events.

### How to Price Memberships

Start with your court booking revenue. If a member books 4 times/month at $25/session, they spend $100/month. Offer a membership at $80/month that includes those 4 bookings plus perks. The member saves money. You get predictable revenue and increased engagement.

### Revenue Potential

A club with 100 members paying an average of $60/month generates $6,000/month in membership revenue — regardless of booking fluctuations.

---

## Revenue Stream 3: Day Passes

Not everyone wants a membership. Day passes capture revenue from casual players, tourists, and visitors.

### How Day Passes Work

**Single-session access.** Pay per visit. No commitment.

**Multi-visit passes.** Buy 5 or 10 visits at a discount.

**Corporate passes.** Sell to businesses for employee wellness programs.

### Pricing Strategy

Day passes should be priced 10-20% above the equivalent per-session membership rate. This incentivizes memberships while capturing revenue from players who won't commit.

### Revenue Potential

A club in a tourist area can generate $2,000-5,000/month from day passes alone. Even non-tourist locations see $500-1,500/month from casual visitors.

## Revenue Stream 4: Pro Shop Sales

Your players need equipment. Buy it from you, not Amazon.

### What to Sell

**Rackets.** Stock a range from beginner to advanced. Offer demo rackets for trial.

**Balls.** Padel balls are consumable. Players need them regularly.

**Grips and accessories.** Overgrips, wristbands, bags, and other accessories.

**Apparel.** Club-branded clothing, shoes, and accessories.

### How to Sell

**In-store POS.** Sell at the front desk through PlayGeko's POS system.

**Online store.** Players order through the app. Pick up at the club or get delivery.

**Coaching recommendations.** Coaches recommend equipment to players during lessons.

### Revenue Potential

A well-run pro shop generates $1,000-3,000/month. The margin on accessories (grips, balls, bags) is typically 40-60%.

## Revenue Stream 5: Events & Tournaments

Events bring the community together and generate significant one-time revenue.

### Event Types

**Tournaments.** Entry fees, spectator tickets, sponsorships, and food/beverage sales.

**Social events.** Mixers, holiday parties, and community gatherings. Ticket sales and sponsorships.

**Corporate events.** Team-building sessions for businesses. Premium pricing.

**Clinics.** Guest coaches, specialty workshops, and intensive training sessions.

### How to Monetize Events

**Entry fees.** $20-50 per player for tournaments. Scale based on prestige and prizes.

**Sponsorships.** Local businesses sponsor events for branding. $500-2,000 per event.

**Food and beverage.** Partner with a vendor or run your own. 20-30% margin on F&B.

**Merchandise.** Sell event-specific merchandise (t-shirts, bags, etc.).

### Revenue Potential

A monthly tournament with 32 players at $30 entry generates $960 in entry fees alone. Add sponsorships, F&B, and merchandise, and a single event can generate $2,000-4,000.

## Revenue Stream 6: Facility Rentals

Your courts have value beyond your regular programming.

### Rental Opportunities

**Private events.** Birthday parties, corporate events, and private tournaments.

**Film and photo shoots.** Padel courts make visually striking locations.

**Camps and clinics.** Rent courts to external coaches and academies for intensive programs.

**Multi-sport events.** If you have multiple court types, offer combined packages.

### Pricing Strategy

Facility rentals should be priced at a premium over regular court bookings — typically 50-100% higher. You're selling exclusivity and dedicated access.

### Revenue Potential

A club that hosts 4 private events per month at $500 each generates $2,000/month in rental revenue. This is often pure margin since it fills otherwise empty courts.

## FAQ

### How much can coaching increase my revenue?

Clubs that implement coaching packages see 20-30% increases in total revenue within the first quarter. Coaching typically represents 25-35% of total revenue for well-run clubs.
