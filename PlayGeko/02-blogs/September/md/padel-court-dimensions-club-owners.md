## Introduction

Whether you're building a new padel facility or renovating an existing one, court dimensions matter. Wrong measurements mean wrong builds. Wrong builds mean wasted money.

This guide covers official padel court dimensions, spacing requirements, and what club owners need to know about facility planning.

---

## Official Padel Court Dimensions

### Playing Area

- **Length:** 20 meters (65 feet 7 inches)
- **Width:** 10 meters (32 feet 10 inches)
- **Total area:** 200 square meters (2,153 square feet)

### Net Height

- **Center:** 0.88 meters (2 feet 10.6 inches)
- **Posts:** 1.05 meters (3 feet 5.4 inches)

### Service Boxes

- **Length:** 6.95 meters (22 feet 10 inches) from the net
- **Width:** Half the court width (5 meters / 16 feet 5 inches)

### Wall Heights

- **Back walls:** 4 meters (13 feet 1 inch) — glass panels
- **Side walls:** 3 meters (9 feet 10 inches) — mesh fencing
- **Corner walls:** 2 meters (6 feet 7 inches) — glass panels

---

## Spacing Requirements

### Between Courts

- **Minimum:** 1 meter (3 feet 3 inches) between parallel courts
- **Recommended:** 2 meters (6 feet 7 inches) for player safety and comfort

### Behind Courts

- **Minimum:** 2 meters (6 feet 7 inches) behind back walls
- **Recommended:** 3-4 meters for spectator seating and movement

### Side Clearance

- **Minimum:** 1 meter (3 feet 3 inches) from side walls to any obstruction
- **Recommended:** 2 meters for player safety during wide shots

## Facility Planning Considerations

### Indoor vs. Outdoor

**Indoor facilities:**
- Ceiling height: minimum 8 meters (26 feet 3 inches) recommended
- Lighting: 300-500 lux for recreational, 500-750 lux for competitive
- Climate control: ventilation and temperature management

**Outdoor facilities:**
- Wind protection: windbreaks or screening on exposed sides
- Drainage: proper drainage around courts
- Lighting: LED floodlights for evening play

### Court Surface

**Artificial turf:**
- Most common surface for padel
- Sand-filled or sand-dressed
- Requires regular brushing and sand distribution

**Concrete:**
- Less common but durable
- Harder on player joints
- Lower maintenance

### Court Orientation

- **North-south alignment** preferred to avoid sun in players' eyes
- **East-west alignment** possible with proper screening
- **Wind direction** should be considered for outdoor courts

## Cost Implications

### Construction Costs

- **Basic court:** $30,000-50,000 per court (excluding land)
- **Premium court:** $50,000-80,000 per court (glass walls, lighting, surfaces)
- **Indoor facility:** $100,000-200,000+ per court (building, climate control)

### Space Requirements

- **Minimum facility:** 4 courts = 1,000+ square meters (10,764 square feet)
- **Recommended facility:** 4 courts = 1,500+ square meters (16,146 square feet)
- **With amenities:** Add 20-30% for reception, pro shop, changing rooms

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Wrong Spacing

Building courts too close together creates safety issues and reduces player comfort. Follow minimum spacing requirements.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Ceiling Height

Indoor facilities with low ceilings limit playability. 8 meters minimum recommended.

### Mistake 3: Poor Orientation

Courts oriented east-west cause sun glare issues. North-south is preferred.

### Mistake 4: Cutting Corners on Glass

Back wall glass must be tempered safety glass. Regular glass is dangerous and non-compliant.

### Mistake 5: Forgetting Amenities

Players need changing rooms, showers, and a reception area. Don't build courts without them.
