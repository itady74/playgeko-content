## The Multi-Sport Challenge

### Operational Complexity

**Different court types.** Padel, tennis, squash, basketball — each with different dimensions and requirements.

**Different equipment.** Rackets, balls, nets — sport-specific inventory.

**Different schedules.** Each sport has its own peak times and patterns.

**Different communities.** Each sport has its own player culture.

### Benefits

**Cross-sport memberships.** Players try multiple sports.

**Shared resources.** Common areas, staff, and equipment.

**Diverse revenue.** Multiple revenue streams reduce risk.

**Year-round activity.** Different sports peak at different times.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Treating Each Sport Independently

Sports shouldn't operate in silos. Cross-sport integration creates value.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Cross-Sport Opportunities

Members who play multiple sports are more valuable. Encourage exploration.

### Mistake 3: Complex Pricing

If players can't understand your pricing, they won't play. Keep it simple.

### Mistake 4: Poor Communication

Players should know about all offerings. Cross-promote effectively.

### Mistake 5: No Unified Experience

Players shouldn't feel like they're using different systems for different sports. One platform, one experience.
