## Introduction

Tournaments fill courts. They build community. They generate revenue. And they put your club on the map.

But running a tournament isn't as simple as setting up a bracket and letting players play. Poor organization kills tournaments.

This guide walks you through every step of running a successful padel tournament, from planning to execution.

---

## Step 1: Define Your Tournament

### Format Selection

**Single elimination.** Fast, exciting, one loss and you're out. Best for large fields with limited time.

**Double elimination.** More forgiving. Players get a second chance in the loser's bracket. Better for competitive events.

**Round robin.** Everyone plays everyone in their group. Top players advance. Best for smaller groups and social tournaments.

**Swiss system.** Players paired based on record after each round. Good for large fields without elimination.

### Categories

**Open.** No restrictions. Best players compete.

**Level-based.** Divide by skill level (1.0-6.0 scale). Fairer competition.

**Age-based.** Junior, senior, masters categories.

**Gender-based.** Men's, women's, mixed doubles.

**Corporate.** Business teams competing.

### Size and Schedule

**Number of teams:** 8, 16, 32, or 64 teams.

**Duration:** 1 day (small), 2 days (medium), 1 week (large).

**Match length:** Best of 1 set, best of 3 sets, or time-based.

---

## Step 2: Set Rules and Regulations

### Match Rules

**Scoring:** Standard padel scoring (15, 30, 40, deuce).

**Tiebreak:** First to 7 points (2 clear) at 6-6.

**Sets:** Best of 1 or best of 3 (define clearly).

**Time limits:** If time-based, specify how ties are broken.

### Tournament Rules

**Check-in:** Players must check in 15-30 minutes before match.

**Late policy:** 10-minute grace period, then forfeit.

**Substitutes:** Allow or disallow substitutes.

**Warm-up:** 5 minutes maximum per match.

**Disputes:** Define how disputes are resolved (referee, tournament director).

### Prizes

**Winner:** Trophy, prize money, free coaching, membership extension.

**Runner-up:** Trophy, prize money, membership extension.

**Best player:** Special award for outstanding performance.

**Participation:** Certificate or small gift for all participants.

## Step 3: Registration and Payment

### Registration Process

**Online registration.** Use PlayGeko or tournament management software.

**Information required:**
- Player name and contact
- Skill level (self-assessed or verified)
- Partner name (for doubles)
- Emergency contact
- Payment

### Payment Options

**Entry fee:** $20-100 per team (market dependent).

**Payment methods:** Online payment, bank transfer, cash at venue.

**Refund policy:** Full refund 7+ days before. 50% refund 3-7 days. No refund within 3 days.

### Team Management

**Individual registration.** Players register solo, organizers pair them.

**Team registration.** Pre-formed teams register together.

**Waitlist.** When full, maintain a waitlist for cancellations.

## Step 4: Scheduling

### Court Allocation

**Number of courts.** How many courts are available?

**Match duration.** How long per match? Add buffer time.

**Rest time.** How long between matches for the same team?

### Schedule Creation

**Round times.** When does each round start?

**Court assignments.** Which matches on which courts?

**Buffer time.** 15-30 minutes between matches for court prep and rest.

### Example Schedule (16 Teams, 4 Courts)

**Day 1:**
- 9:00-10:30 — Round 1 (4 matches)
- 10:45-12:15 — Round 2 (4 matches)
- 13:30-15:00 — Round 3 (4 matches)
- 15:15-16:45 — Round 4 (4 matches)

**Day 2:**
- 9:00-10:30 — Quarterfinals (4 matches)
- 11:00-12:30 — Semifinals (2 matches)
- 14:00-15:30 — Third place match
- 16:00-17:30 — Final

## Step 5: Communication

### Pre-Tournament

**Registration confirmation.** Confirm entry, payment, and details.

**Tournament rules.** Send complete rules and schedule.

**Court assignments.** Inform players which courts to use.

**Reminders.** Send reminders 1 week, 1 day, and morning of.

### During Tournament

**Live updates.** Post results as matches finish.

**Bracket updates.** Keep brackets current.

**Schedule changes.** Communicate any delays or changes immediately.

**Social media.** Share photos, videos, and highlights.

### Post-Tournament

**Results announcement.** Official results and rankings.

**Thank you message.** Thank participants, sponsors, and staff.

**Feedback survey.** Collect feedback for improvement.

**Photo/video sharing.** Share tournament highlights.

## Step 6: Operations

### Staff Requirements

**Tournament director.** Overall coordination.

**Referees.** 1 per 2-4 courts (depending on level).

**Scorekeepers.** 1 per court (or use technology).

**Court maintenance.** Staff to prepare courts between matches.

**Registration desk.** Check-in and information.

### Equipment

**Scoreboards.** Digital or manual scorekeeping.

**Balls.** Plenty of fresh balls.

**First aid.** First aid kit and trained staff.

**Water and snacks.** Player refreshments.

**Branding.** Tournament banners, signage, and decorations.

### Technology

**Live scoring.** Digital scorekeeping with live updates.

**Bracket management.** Software for bracket creation and updates.

**Communication.** WhatsApp group or tournament app.

**Photography.** Capture moments for social media.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Poor Scheduling

Too many matches in a short time. Not enough rest. Court conflicts. Plan carefully.

### Mistake 2: Inadequate Communication

Players don't know when/where to play. Results aren't posted. Keep everyone informed.

### Mistake 3: Insufficient Staffing

Not enough referees, scorekeepers, or support. Understaffing kills tournaments.

### Mistake 4: Ignoring Player Experience

No shade, no water, no seating. Players remember bad experiences.

### Mistake 5: No Follow-Up

Tournament ends, no results published, no photos shared. Missed community-building opportunity.

## FAQ

### How many teams do I need for a tournament?

Minimum 4 teams for a meaningful tournament. 8, 16, or 32 teams are common sizes.

### How long does a tournament take?

Small tournament (8 teams): 1 day. Medium (16 teams): 2 days. Large (32+ teams): 3-7 days.

### What's the best format for beginners?

Round robin or time-based formats are best for beginners. Less pressure, more play.

### How do I handle disputes?

Define a clear dispute resolution process. Designate a tournament director to make final decisions.
