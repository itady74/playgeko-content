## Introduction

Pricing is the most powerful lever in your padel club's revenue strategy. Set prices too high, courts stay empty. Set them too low, you leave money on the table.

The answer isn't one price for all hours. It's a pricing strategy that matches demand with value.

This guide shows you how to structure padel court pricing that maximizes revenue without scaring players away.

---

## The Pricing Framework

### Three Pricing Zones

**Peak hours.** Friday evening, Saturday morning, Sunday afternoon. Highest demand. Highest prices.

**Standard hours.** Weekday evenings, weekend afternoons. Moderate demand. Standard prices.

**Off-peak hours.** Weekday mornings, late evenings. Lowest demand. Lowest prices.

### Price Ratios

A common framework:
- **Off-peak:** 1.0x (base price)
- **Standard:** 1.3-1.5x
- **Peak:** 1.8-2.2x

Example: If your base price is $25/hour:
- Off-peak: $25
- Standard: $33-37
- Peak: $45-55

---

## Setting Your Base Price

### Factors to Consider

**Market rates.** What do other clubs in your area charge? Don't undercut dramatically — it signals low quality.

**Your costs.** Court maintenance, staff, utilities, rent. Your base price must cover costs with margin.

**Player perception.** Price too low and players question quality. Price too high and they shop elsewhere.

**Court quality.** Glass courts, premium surfaces, and amenities justify higher prices.

### Research Method

1. Call 3-5 competing clubs. Ask their rates.
2. Calculate your cost per court-hour (rent + maintenance + staff + utilities).
3. Set base price at 2-3x your cost per hour.
4. Adjust based on market rates and perceived value.

## Peak Hour Pricing

### When Are Peak Hours?

**Friday evening (5-9 PM).** Highest demand. Everyone wants to play after work.

**Saturday morning (9 AM-12 PM).** Weekend prime time.

**Sunday afternoon (2-6 PM).** Family and social play time.

### How to Price Peak Hours

**Premium pricing.** 1.8-2.2x your base rate. Players expect to pay more for prime time.

**Limited discounts.** No membership discounts during peak. No promotional pricing.

**Minimum booking time.** Require 90-minute bookings during peak to maximize revenue per slot.

### The Revenue Impact

A club with 6 courts running at 80% occupancy during peak hours generates significantly more revenue than one running at 50% occupancy with flat pricing. Peak pricing captures the value players place on prime time.

---

## Off-Peak Pricing

### When Are Off-Peak Hours?

**Weekday mornings (6 AM-12 PM).** Courts sit empty. Workers are at offices.

**Late evenings (9-11 PM).** Most players have gone home.

**Mid-afternoon (2-4 PM).** Between lunch and after-work rush.

### How to Price Off-Peak Hours

**Discounted rates.** 0.7-0.9x your base rate. Fill empty courts.

**Membership incentives.** Offer off-peak memberships at reduced rates.

**Group discounts.** Encourage groups to book during slow times.

**Corporate packages.** Target businesses for team-building during off-peak.

### The Fill Rate Goal

Off-peak courts should aim for 50-60% occupancy. That's revenue from otherwise empty courts. Even at discounted rates, revenue > no revenue.

## Dynamic Pricing

### What Is Dynamic Pricing?

Dynamic pricing adjusts prices in real-time based on demand, weather, and booking patterns.

### How It Works

**High demand = higher prices.** When courts are filling fast, prices increase.

**Low demand = lower prices.** When courts are empty, prices decrease.

**Weather adjustment.** Rain forecast = lower outdoor prices. Sunshine = higher prices.

**Event-driven.** Local events affecting demand trigger automatic price adjustments.

## Membership Pricing

### Tiered Membership Structure

**Basic membership.** Access to booking platform, marketplace listing, member-only events. $20-30/month.

**Premium membership.** Discounted court rates, priority booking, free coaching assessment. $50-70/month.

**All-access membership.** Unlimited booking (within fair-use limits), coaching discounts, tournament entry. $100-150/month.

### The Membership Math

If a member books 4 times/month at $25/session, they spend $100/month. Offer a membership at $80/month that includes those 4 bookings plus perks. The member saves $20. You get predictable revenue and increased engagement.

## Day Pass Pricing

### What Are Day Passes?

Single-session access for casual players, tourists, and visitors. No commitment.

### Pricing Strategy

**Above membership rate.** Day passes should be 10-20% above the equivalent per-session membership rate.

**Multi-visit passes.** Buy 5 or 10 visits at a discount. Incentivizes repeat visits.

**Corporate passes.** Sell to businesses for employee wellness programs.

---

## Seasonal Pricing

### Adjusting for Seasons

**Summer (outdoor courts).** Higher prices during good weather. Peak demand.

**Winter (indoor courts).** Maintain or slightly increase prices. Indoor courts are premium during cold months.

**Holiday periods.** Adjust for school holidays, public holidays, and local events.

**Ramadan/special periods.** Adjust hours and pricing for cultural observances in your market.

---

## Pricing Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Flat Pricing All Day

One price for all hours leaves money on the table during peak and fails to fill courts during off-peak.

### Mistake 2: Racing to the Bottom

Undercutting competitors destroys margins and signals low quality. Compete on value, not price.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Costs

Price below your cost per court-hour and you lose money on every booking.

### Mistake 4: No Dynamic Adjustment

Fixed prices don't respond to demand changes. You miss revenue during high demand and fail to fill courts during low demand.

### Mistake 5: Complex Pricing

If players can't understand your pricing in 10 seconds, it's too complicated. Keep it simple.
